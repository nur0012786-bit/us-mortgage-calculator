Mortgage Payment Breakdown: What Is Included?

A mortgage payment is often more than principal and interest. Understanding every component makes it easier to compare a calculator estimate with a lender's Loan Estimate.

The five common components

Principal is the amount of the loan balance you repay. Interest is the lender's charge for borrowing the money. Together, principal and interest are the core loan payment.

Property taxes and homeowners insurance may be collected monthly through an escrow account. PMI may apply to some conventional mortgages when the down payment is below 20%. HOA dues are a separate housing cost for many communities and may not be paid to the mortgage servicer.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that a total monthly payment can include principal, interest, mortgage insurance and escrowed taxes and insurance. See the CFPB's mortgage payment guidance for the official explanation.

Why the number can surprise homebuyers

Suppose a calculator shows $1,900 for principal and interest. That does not automatically mean your housing payment will be $1,900. Property taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance and HOA dues can raise the amount you need to budget each month.

Taxes and insurance can also change over time, so a fixed-rate mortgage does not necessarily mean your total housing payment is fixed forever.

How to compare your estimate with a lender

Once you receive a Loan Estimate, compare the loan amount, monthly principal and interest, mortgage insurance, estimated taxes and insurance, closing costs and cash to close. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau recommends reviewing these figures rather than comparing only the advertised principal-and-interest payment.

Use the calculator

Enter your home price, down payment, rate and term in the US mortgage calculator. Then open the advanced costs section to model taxes, insurance, PMI and HOA fees separately.
