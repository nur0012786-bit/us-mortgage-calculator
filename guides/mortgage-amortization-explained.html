Mortgage Amortization Explained

An amortization schedule shows how each scheduled payment changes the loan balance. Each payment is divided between interest and principal.

What changes over time?

Interest is calculated from the outstanding balance. As the balance declines, the interest portion generally declines and more of the scheduled payment goes to principal.

Why total interest matters

Two loans with the same home price can have very different lifetime costs because rate and term affect the amount of interest paid. A shorter term usually means higher scheduled payments but fewer payments and less interest, all else equal.

Extra payments

Additional principal payments can reduce the balance sooner. Whether that is useful depends on the loan terms, opportunity cost and other financial priorities. Check for any applicable prepayment restrictions.
