20% Down Payment and PMI: What the Threshold Means

A 20% down payment is an important planning benchmark, but it should not be treated as a universal rule for every loan.

Why 20% matters

For many conventional mortgages, a down payment below 20% can result in private mortgage insurance. The CFPB describes mortgage insurance as a cost that may be required when the down payment is less than 20%, depending on the loan. citeturn1search2

At 20% down, the initial loan-to-value ratio is 80%. That means a $400,000 purchase with $80,000 down starts with a $320,000 loan. A smaller down payment produces a larger loan and may also produce a mortgage-insurance cost.

20% is not the same as “the lender requires 20%”

Loan programs can have different down-payment requirements. Some borrowers use lower down payments, while other programs have their own mortgage-insurance or funding-fee structures. Your lender and loan program determine the actual rules.

What to compare when choosing a down payment

  • Monthly principal and interest.
  • Estimated mortgage insurance.
  • Cash remaining after closing.
  • Closing costs and prepaid expenses.
  • Your emergency savings and other financial goals.

Putting more cash into the home can reduce the loan balance, but using every available dollar for the down payment can leave less liquidity. The useful comparison is the whole financial picture, not just the PMI line.

Use the down payment calculator

Our down payment calculator lets you test different down-payment percentages and see the estimated loan amount and modeled PMI. The main mortgage calculator lets you model taxes, insurance and HOA costs as well.

Important PMI limitation

PMI rules vary by loan and lender. Our main calculator uses a simplified estimate based on the loan balance reaching 80% of the original home value. It is not a statement of your lender's cancellation policy.
