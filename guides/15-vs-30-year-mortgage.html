LOAN TERM GUIDE

15-Year vs. 30-Year Mortgage: Payment & Interest Tradeoffs

The shorter term usually requires a higher monthly principal-and-interest payment, while the longer term spreads repayment over more months.

What changes when the term changes?

For the same loan balance and interest rate, a 15-year fixed mortgage has half as many scheduled monthly payments as a 30-year mortgage. Because the balance is repaid faster, the monthly principal-and-interest payment is higher. The shorter schedule can also result in less total interest over the life of the loan.

The exact difference depends on the rate and loan amount. Taxes, insurance, PMI and HOA costs can be added separately and may not change in the same way as principal and interest.

Why monthly payment is only one part of the comparison

A 30-year term can provide a lower scheduled payment, which may leave more monthly cash available for savings or other goals. A 15-year term commits the borrower to a higher scheduled payment but shortens the repayment period.

When comparing the two, consider the payment you can sustain, the total interest, the opportunity cost of using more cash for the mortgage, and your broader financial plan.

Run the same scenario both ways

Use the US mortgage calculator with the same home price, down payment and rate, then switch the term between 15 and 30 years. Compare the monthly principal-and-interest payment, total interest and scheduled payoff.

Example framework

Imagine a $320,000 loan. The useful comparison is not a universal dollar figure because the rate matters. Instead, keep the loan amount and rate constant, calculate both terms, and record three outputs: monthly payment, total interest and payoff date.
