HOME AFFORDABILITY GUIDE

How Much House Can I Afford?

A useful home budget starts with the total monthly housing cost you can sustain, not just the mortgage principal and interest.

Start with your full housing budget

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau recommends thinking about the total monthly home payment, including principal, interest, property taxes, mortgage insurance when applicable, homeowners insurance and HOA fees. It also recommends budgeting for maintenance, repairs and utilities rather than treating the mortgage as the entire cost of owning a home.

That means an affordability estimate should leave room for costs outside the loan itself. A number that looks comfortable when you consider only principal and interest can become much tighter once the rest of the housing budget is included.

A practical calculation

  1. Start with gross monthly household income.
  2. Choose a target housing ratio or debt-to-income assumption for planning.
  3. Subtract recurring monthly debt payments.
  4. Reserve part of the housing budget for property taxes and homeowners insurance.
  5. Account for PMI or HOA costs when applicable.
  6. Use the remaining amount to estimate the loan payment and corresponding home price.

There is no single universal home-price number that works for every household. Credit profile, loan program, reserves, rate, taxes, insurance and lender underwriting can change the amount a borrower qualifies for.

Why a lender's maximum is not necessarily your personal budget

A lender evaluates repayment risk using its underwriting rules. Your personal budget also needs to account for savings goals, emergency reserves, childcare, transportation, utilities, maintenance and other commitments. The two numbers can therefore be different.
