Mortgage Refinance Break-Even: How to Calculate It

A refinance can change the interest rate, payment, term and upfront costs. Break-even analysis helps organize the comparison, but it should not be the only measure.

The simple break-even formula

A basic calculation is:

Break-even months = refinance closing costs ÷ monthly payment savings

For example, if estimated refinance costs are $6,000 and the new payment is $200 lower, the simple break-even point is 30 months. This is only a planning calculation; it does not account for every difference between the old and new loans.

Why the simple number can mislead

A refinance can reset the loan term. A lower monthly payment can therefore come from stretching repayment over more years rather than from a lower total borrowing cost. Conversely, a shorter new term can raise the payment while reducing the time the debt remains outstanding.

Other items can matter too, including points, prepaid interest, changes in mortgage insurance, taxes and insurance, cash paid at closing, and whether the borrower expects to keep the property long enough to recover the upfront costs.

Compare more than the monthly payment

  • Current loan balance and rate.
  • Remaining term on the current mortgage.
  • New rate and new term.
  • Refinance closing costs and lender credits.
  • Change in monthly principal and interest.
  • Total interest under each scenario.
  • How long you expect to keep the new loan.
